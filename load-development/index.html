Three ways to keep a load log

Load development is only as good as the notes you can find later. Here's how the usual ways hold up when you need last spring's ladder.

The notebook

Handwritten logs

  • Hard to find. Which notebook, which page, which rifle? Was that before or after the new barrel?
  • Math by hand. Averages, SD and ES on a calculator at the bench, or not at all.
  • Groups live somewhere else. The targets are in a box, if you kept them, and nothing ties a target to its rung.
  • One copy. Rain, a coffee spill or a lost range bag, and the test is gone.
The spreadsheet

Better, still annoying

  • Searchable, once you're home. At the range you're still writing on the box and typing it up later.
  • Formulas break. Add a reading and the SD cell doesn't reach it. Every ladder needs its own copy of the sheet.
  • Groups are just numbers. A size in a cell, without the shots, the distance or the load it came from.
  • Two places for one answer. The sheet holds the result, but your solver doesn't use it. Pick a winner and you still type the charge and velocity into your ballistics app by hand, and do it again every time you update one of them.
Bullseye Ballistics

Built for the bench and the range

  • Filed by rifle, with the test type, the charges or depths and the rungs fired on every row.
  • No math by hand. Copy the velocities from your chronograph's app and paste them into the rung. The app works out the average, SD and ES for you.
  • Real groups on every rung: a photo of the target measured in the app, or the group's dimensions typed in. Saved for later analysis.
  • Brass firings kept with every test, so you know how many times the brass had been fired when you shot it.
  • One tap to a load. Use this step writes the charge, seating and velocity into your load and the solver.
How it works

From first rung to finished load

Set up

Pick the test type and rifle, enter starting point, ending point and desired step size, and let the app build the rungs.

Shoot

Paste each rung's chronograph readings and add the groups you shot with it: a target photo or the dimensions.

Compare

Velocity and group size for every rung, side by side on one chart.

Use it

Turn the rung you choose into a new load, or update one you have.

Load Dev is on the Home screen. You can also start a workup from any rifle: open it in Profiles and tap New workup under Workups.

Walkthrough

Start a workup in three steps

A workup is one ladder test. Setting one up takes under a minute, and the X at the top closes it without saving.

What you're testing

  • Charge ladder: the same components, a different powder charge on each rung. Shot over a chronograph, it's your velocity ladder too.
  • Seating depth: the same charge, a different seating length on each rung, measured as CBTO or COAL.
  • Pick the rifle and give the test a name, or leave the name blank.

What stays the same

Enter the parts of the recipe that don't change from rung to rung: powder, bullet, primer, brass and how many times it's been fired, case prep, seating length and distance to target. Leave out anything you don't track.

Brass firings: the Firings box next to Brass records how many times that brass has been fired. The test shows it with the brass, such as Alpha ×4 for Alpha brass on its fourth firing, so you can compare results on new brass and well-used brass.

These stay with the test, so a year from now you know exactly what was in the case.

Let the app do the math

  • Fill between: type the first charge, the last and the step. 42.0 to 43.2 gr in 0.3 gr steps gives five rungs: 42.0, 42.3, 42.6, 42.9 and 43.2.
  • Add one adds a single rung anywhere in the list. Tap the X on a chip to drop one.
  • Create workup and the rungs are ready for readings. You can add more rungs later.
Walkthrough

Record each rung at the range

Tap a rung to open it.

  • Paste readings straight from your chronograph app. Spaces, commas or new lines all work. Or type them one at a time.
  • The stats fill in: average velocity, SD, ES and the number of shots. Remove a reading and they update.
  • SD with a range. Under the SD you see how far the true SD could be, at 95% confidence. Ten shots at SD 5.8 could really be anywhere from 4.0 to 10.5. More shots narrow it, so you know when a rung's numbers are solid and when they're luck.
  • When it was shot keeps the date and the ammo and air temperature with the test.
Walkthrough

The groups you actually shot, on every rung

Velocity is half the story. Bullseye Ballistics stores the actual groups you fired with each rung, not just a number you wrote in the margin. Take a photo of the target and measure the group in the app, or enter its dimensions. Either way it's saved with the rung, so they're there to compare now and to analyze later.

On the rung

New group or Link a group

At the bottom of a rung, New group records a group for it. Link a group attaches one you've already saved in Groups. Two ways to record it:

  • Photo: photograph the target and mark the impacts. The app measures the group.
  • Dimensions: enter each impact by hand, or type in the group size and mean radius from another tool.
Fair comparison

The mean, not the best

Shoot two or three groups per rung and the rung shows the mean group size, so one lucky group doesn't stand for the whole rung. A linked group stops counting toward a load's spread while it belongs to the test.

For later

Kept for analysis

Each group keeps its shots, size and mean radius, and its target photo stays on your phone. Sagitta reads every rung's readings and groups when you ask about a workup, and the measurements are in your backup and data export.

Walkthrough

Compare the rungs at a glance

Open the workup and the Rungs compared card puts the whole ladder on one axis.

  • Velocity: each rung's average, with bars of plus or minus 1 SD. Look for rungs where the velocity barely moves as the charge goes up.
  • Group size: each rung's mean group in mil, right under its velocity.
  • The rungs list gives the numbers behind each point: charge, velocity, SD and its range, ES, shot count and group size.
  • Your call. The points aren't joined by a trend line and the app doesn't announce a node. You see the data as it is and decide, or ask Sagitta for a second opinion.
Ask Sagitta

A second opinion on your ladder

Tap Ask Sagitta on a workup and Sagitta Ballistics AI opens with the test already in view. It reads every rung's readings, stats and groups from your records, not from a summary you type in.

Which rung looks best?

Weighs velocity spread and group size together, rung by rung.

Is there a flat spot?

Looks for charges where velocity holds steady.

What should I test next?

Can offer a card that adds up to five rungs, or a note to the workup.

Stays inside your data

Suggested charges stay within the range you've already fired. Never above your top rung.

Walkthrough

Use this step: from rung to load

Found your rung? Open it and tap Use this step. Choose New load, named after the workup and the rung, or Update a load you already have.

  • The recipe: a charge ladder writes the rung's charge (and the seating length, if you recorded one). A seating test writes the seating length and its measure, plus the charge.
  • The velocity: the rung's average becomes the load's muzzle velocity, so the solver uses what you measured.
  • The temperature table moves to the new velocity and keeps its slope.
  • A fresh start where it matters: truing starts over, and cold-bore and clean-bore velocities are cleared when the recipe changes, because the old numbers belonged to a different load.
  • A velocity check is saved from the rung's readings, and the rung remembers which load it became.
After the workup

Keep checking velocity as the barrel wears

A load isn't finished when the ladder is. Barrels speed up or slow down over their life. Velocity checks on each load keep a running record, and every check made on the day it's shot is stamped with the barrel and its round count.

Use measured MV spread

Your real velocity spread

With 10 readings across your checks, hit probability uses your load's measured SD instead of the general setting.

Adjust MV for wear

Velocity that follows the barrel

With three or more checks on the current barrel, the app fits velocity against round count, allowing for temperature. When the change is real, not noise, it adjusts your MV for today's count, for example: -2.1 fps per 100 rounds from 3 checks. Adjusting your MV by -12 fps now. Past your last check it holds steady and tells you it's time for another velocity check.

Your load history

Find it in seconds, years later

The point of keeping records is using them. Every workup stays filed under its rifle, with everything that went into it.

  • Load Dev lists every workup by rifle: the test type, the range of charges or depths, and how many rungs were fired.
  • Each rifle shows its workups next to its barrel and cleaning card, with the rounds on the barrel and life left.
  • Open any test and it's all there: components, date, temperatures, every reading, every group, and which loads came from it.
  • Velocity drifting? Your old ladders are one tap away, with their readings and groups, ready to plan the next test from. Or ask Sagitta to compare them.
  • Backed up. Workups and velocity checks are in the free data export, and sync to your account with cloud backup.
Good to know

The details

Big ladders

Fill between builds up to 25 rungs at a time. Need more? Use a larger step, or add rungs to the workup later.

Works offline

Record readings and groups with no signal. Sagitta and cloud backup catch up when you're connected.

Safety first

The app records your tests. It doesn't suggest maximum charges. Always start from published load data and watch for pressure signs.
