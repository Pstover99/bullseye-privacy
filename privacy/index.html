Privacy Policy for Bullseye Ballistics

Last updated: October 26, 2026

Bullseye Ballistics ("Bullseye," "the app") is a long-range shooting ballistics calculator developed by Core Bridge, LLC. ("we," "us"). This policy explains what the app accesses on your device and how that information is handled. In short: Bullseye works on your device. There are two exceptions, both optional: with an account and Premium you can back up your data so it survives a lost phone, and with an account and a monthly plan you can ask Sagitta, the app's AI assistant, about your shooting. Without an account, the app sends none of your data anywhere. We show no ads, we use no analytics or tracking, and we do not sell or share your data.

Information the app accesses

Bullseye does not require an account, and every feature but Sagitta and cloud backup works without one, Premium included. If you choose to create one for cloud backup it asks for an email address and a password, or uses your Google account if you prefer that — see Cloud backup below. To provide its features, the app may use the following device capabilities, processed on your device except where noted below:

  • Location (GPS). Used to compute the Coriolis effect and magnetic declination for a more accurate firing solution, and to record the conditions at the time of a logged shot. The app records your latitude and the direction you were firing (azimuth), whether they come from GPS and the compass or you type them. It does not record your longitude. If you switch on cloud backup, the latitude and azimuth saved with each logged shot, and with each saved load, are backed up with it to your own account (see Cloud backup), so a second phone or a restore can use them. If you ask Sagitta, the app's AI assistant, about your shots or a firing solution, the latitude and azimuth go with your question (see Sagitta below), and with cloud backup on they are kept in that conversation in your backup. If your phone's own backup is on, Android also keeps a copy of them in your Google account, as it does for the rest of the app's data. They are sent nowhere else.
  • Bluetooth and nearby devices. Used only to connect to optional environmental sensors (such as anemometers and weather meters) so the app can read live wind, temperature, pressure, and humidity. On some Android versions the operating system requires the location permission in order to scan for Bluetooth devices; the app does not use this permission to track you.
  • Camera and photo picker (optional). The Group Analyzer can measure a shot group from a photograph of the target. Taking the photo hands the job to your device's own camera app — Bullseye does not request the camera permission and has no access to the camera itself. You may instead choose an existing image, which your device's photo picker provides one file at a time; the app has no access to the rest of your photo library. Any image you use is copied into the app's private storage, is never uploaded unless you attach it to a question for Sagitta (below), and is deleted when you delete the group it belongs to.
  • Microphone (optional, off by default). One feature uses it, and it is off unless you turn it on.

    Voice shot calls (Settings → Voice shot calls). When it is on, the app listens while a target solution is on screen so you can say "hit" or "miss low left" instead of tapping. Speech is recognized on your phone, by a model shipped inside the app — no audio is recorded, saved or sent anywhere. The microphone is closed the moment you leave the solution screen or the app goes to the background, and the only thing kept is the shot you called, exactly as if you had tapped it. The app also keeps the words it thought it heard on screen so you can see and undo a mistake; that text is never stored.

    Your voice is never uploaded, because it is never written down in the first place.

    An earlier version also included a hidden diagnostic that did record short audio clips at a shooting range, so we could measure how phone microphones behave around gunfire. That tool has been removed, and any clips it left on your phone are deleted automatically the next time you open the updated app.

  • Cloud backup (optional, off by default). You can create an account — with an email address and a password, or with Google if you prefer — to back up your rifles, loads, shot log, measured groups, matches, load-development records, barrel and cleaning records, settings and conversations with Sagitta so they survive a lost or replaced phone, and to keep two of your own devices in step. You do not need a Google account, and you do not need an account at all to use the app. It is off until you create one, and signing out stops it.

    Backup is part of Premium (see Purchases). It is switched on and off on the Cloud backup screen, and it is off until you turn it on. If Premium ends, backup stops; what is already backed up stays in your account until you delete it.

    If you sign up with an email address we ask you to confirm it, which is what lets you reset a forgotten password later. Your password is stored by our authentication provider in hashed form; we never see it. You can delete the account, and everything backed up to it, from Settings → Account and backup, or follow these instructions if you no longer have the app installed. What is uploaded is the same data the app already stores locally — your rifles, loads, shot log, measured groups, matches, load-development records, barrel and cleaning records, settings and conversations with Sagitta. That includes the latitude and firing direction saved with each logged shot and each saved load: it is what makes a shot's conditions complete on another phone. A conversation includes each question and answer, the records Sagitta read to answer it, and whether you applied or dismissed each change it suggested. Those records can include your latitude, firing direction and the Solution screen's weather, when Sagitta used them. Not uploaded: audio, target photographs, photos or files you sent to Sagitta, your longitude, today's weather and location on the Solution screen (except where a conversation with Sagitta used them), paired Bluetooth devices, and anything about how you use the app. The backup is stored under your own account and no other user can read it. Deleting the account deletes all of it, location included.

    Until this feature the app had no internet permission at all, and this policy said so. That is no longer true and we would rather say it plainly than leave a stale promise standing. Everything else still works with no network connection.

  • Notifications (optional). If you turn on maintenance reminders, the app asks to show notifications and posts them itself, from the phone, when a rifle is due a clean (by rounds, or on a date you set, which the phone checks once a day) or its barrel nears the life you entered. Nothing about them is sent anywhere.

The app does not access your contacts or your photo library at large.

Data stored on your device

Your profiles, shot logs, measured groups, matches, load-development records, barrel and cleaning records, settings, conversations with Sagitta, and sensor readings are stored locally on your device. Target photographs used by the Group Analyzer are stored in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Uninstalling the app removes this data.

Unless you create an account and switch on cloud backup, or ask Sagitta about it (below), the app uploads none of it. With backup on, a copy of your rifles, loads, shot log, groups, matches, load-development records, barrel and cleaning records, settings and conversations with Sagitta is stored under your own account, including the latitude and firing direction saved with each shot and load. Target photographs, audio and your longitude are never part of that copy. Your phone stays the original; the backup is only ever a copy of it.

If your phone's own backup is on, Android also keeps a copy of this data in your Google account. Target photographs and conversations with Sagitta are not part of it.

Sharing features

The app includes optional features that let you share your own data with people you choose, using apps already on your device (for example a messaging app or Bluetooth). Sharing is initiated entirely by you and goes directly to the recipient you select. We do not receive, store, or process shared files.

  • Shot-string export. You can export your shot-string data to a file. By default, location information is removed from exported files; including it is an explicit, optional choice you control.
  • Backup file. "Back up to a file" saves everything the app holds, including the latitude and firing direction saved with each shot. Location is not removed from it. Share a backup file only with someone you would show where you shoot.
  • Group image export. You can share a measured group as an image of your target photo with the measurements drawn on it, or save it to your phone's gallery. The image is re-rendered before it leaves the app, which removes hidden photo metadata such as the GPS coordinates a phone camera may embed — so a shared or saved image does not reveal where it was taken. A copy saved to your gallery is yours: it stays there, like any other photo, if you delete the group or the app.

Sagitta, the AI assistant (optional)

Sagitta answers questions about your rifles, loads and shooting. It is for adults 18 and older. It needs an account and a monthly plan bought in Google Play (see Purchases).

  • What is sent. Each question you ask, with the earlier messages of that conversation, goes to our server and on to Google's Gemini model on Google Cloud, which writes the answer. To answer, Sagitta reads the records it needs from the app, such as a load, a shot string or a group, and those go with the question.
  • Your location. Sagitta uses your latitude and firing direction (azimuth) to account for the Coriolis effect. When it reads a shot string, the latitude and azimuth saved with each shot go with your question. When it works out a firing solution or a hit chance, the latitude and azimuth on your Solution screen go too, if they are filled in. Your longitude is never sent, because the app does not record it. Your device's identifiers are never sent either.
  • Photos and files you attach, such as a target, a chronograph screen or a match book, are not kept in the chat. A photo is sent once, for that answer, and not kept by us. A photo's hidden details, such as where it was taken, are removed before it is sent. A PDF is uploaded to our storage on Google Cloud in the United States and kept only until Sagitta reads it. It is deleted right after the read, and never kept more than a day, even if the read doesn't finish. Deleting your account deletes it too.
  • What we keep. Your plan (see Purchases), how much of each period's allowance you have used, and a record of the Terms of Use you acknowledged (below). We do not keep your questions, answers, the records Sagitta reads, your location, photos or files on our server, apart from an answer you report (below) and a PDF waiting to be read (above). Our server's logs note that a question was asked and what it cost, never what it said.
  • Your conversations stay on your phone, and in your backup when cloud backup is on (see Cloud backup), so a new phone can show them. The backup holds each question and answer, the records Sagitta read for it, and whether you applied or dismissed each change it suggested. Photos and files you attached are not in it. Deleting a conversation, all of them, or your account deletes the backup's copy too.
  • Google processes this data on our behalf as our service provider, and its terms do not allow it to use the data to train its models. Google may keep what is sent, photos included, and the answer for up to 24 hours, to answer later questions faster. If its automated checks flag a question as possible misuse, Google may keep it, with what was sent with it, for up to 90 days so its staff can review it. These copies expire on their own; we cannot delete them sooner.
  • Reports. If you report an answer, the question, the answer, the reason you picked and any comment you add go to our server with your account, so we can look into it. We keep a report for 90 days, then it is deleted.
  • The Terms of Use. The first time you open Sagitta, you tick that you have read and acknowledge the Terms of Use and confirm you are 18 or older. Our server records which version of the Terms you acknowledged, when, that you confirmed you are 18 or older, the app's version, and your account's email address. When the Terms change, you are asked again, and each acknowledgement is recorded. We keep these records as proof that you accepted the Terms.
  • Deleting your account also deletes your plan, usage and reports on our server. We keep a note that the account was closed and the records of the Terms you acknowledged, as proof that you accepted them. Deleting your account stops a plan bought in Google Play from renewing, without a refund for the period already paid.

Data collection and third parties

We do not sell, rent, or trade your personal data, and we do not profile you. The app contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking SDKs — Google Analytics and Crashlytics were removed in August 2026 and are not coming back.

We use these third-party services, and only if you create an account: Google Firebase, which handles sign-in and stores the backup, and, if you use Sagitta, Google Cloud's Vertex AI, which runs the model that writes its answers. Google processes that data on our behalf as our service provider. If you buy a Sagitta plan, Google Play sells it and tells our server its state (see Purchases). Google Play also sells Premium. If you never create an account, none of your data is sent to them or to us.

Purchases

Bullseye Ballistics is free on Google Play. Two optional subscriptions are sold in Google Play, and either can be bought without the other: Premium, which adds features to the app, and Sagitta, the AI assistant, sold as a monthly plan. The app shows no advertising. Our terms say how the plans, the free trial, renewing and canceling work.

Google Play handles every payment; we never see or store your payment details.

Premium needs no account. Google Play keeps the purchase, and the app asks Google Play on your phone whether you have it; the app does not send it to us. So that Premium works offline, the app keeps a small note on your phone: when Google Play last confirmed Premium, and when a free trial or Premium given by hand (below) ends. That note is not part of Android's phone backup. Google Play tells our server when a Premium subscription changes, as it does for Sagitta's plans; our server keeps nothing about it. If we give an account Premium by hand, for a tester, our server keeps when it ends and a short note on who gave it, and the app checks for it about once a day while you are signed in. That record is deleted with your account.

Sagitta's plans need an account. When you buy a plan, the app sends our server Google Play's purchase token, and our server asks Google Play about it. We keep the token and what Google Play says about it: which plan, whether it is in its free trial, its state (such as canceled or on hold), when the current period started and when it renews or ends, and Google Play's order number. Google Play also tells our server when a plan renews, is canceled or ends. Each purchase carries a one-way code made from your account's id, so a plan stays with the Bullseye account that bought it. This record is deleted with your account.

Children

Bullseye is intended for an adult audience and is not directed to children. Sagitta is for adults 18 and older only, and asks you to confirm your age before you first use it.
