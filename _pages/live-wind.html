Live Wind

Understand what's happening.

Let the solver do the math. You read the conditions and make the shot. Live Wind streams the wind direction and speed into the solver: what the wind is doing now, plus the high, low and average from the last two minutes. Your bracket identified, and your hold within it, calculated and on screen.

A wind clock that shows the whole picture.

Your target sits at 12 o'clock. The blue arrow shows where the wind is coming from right now. The shaded arc shows how far it has swung over the last two minutes. A quick label like "Switchy headwind" tells you what kind of wind you're dealing with before you read a single number.

03

Wind speed you can read at a glance.

A vertical speed bar shows the wind right now, along with the High, Average and Low over the last two minutes. Each reading has its own box with its speed and the exact hold it calls for, and the current and average winds show where they're coming from. The bar rescales on its own as conditions change, from a 3 mph breeze to a 20 mph blow, so the readings never pile up or run off the scale.

Why doesn’t the strongest wind need the most hold?

In this example, the highest wind speed (7.6 mph) doesn’t produce the largest hold. That’s not a mistake. The solver accounts for wind direction, not just speed.

Only the part of the wind blowing across the bullet’s path moves it left or right. A wind straight from 12 o’clock (a pure headwind) pushes almost nothing sideways, while a wind from 3 or 9 o’clock is “full value” and pushes the most. Even a few degrees off dead-center makes a measurable difference at long range.

Here, the 7.6 mph gust came almost straight down the line, so it needed only 0.28 mil. The lighter 6.3 mph current wind is also shown “from 12:00,” since directions are rounded to the nearest half hour on the clock, but it was angled slightly more across the bullet’s path, so it needs 0.37 mil, the largest hold on the screen.

You might also wonder why a near-headwind needs any hold at all. That’s spin drift: the bullet’s rotation causes it to drift slightly to one side over a long flight, regardless of wind. The solver folds spin drift into every hold, so even a dead-on headwind shows a small correction.

The takeaway: wind speed alone doesn’t tell you your hold. Direction matters just as much, and the solver does that math for you on every reading, spin drift included - all while you're behind the rifle, in the scope, and NOT breaking position.

Reading the Live Wind display

    Wind hold: your firing solution Uses everything: your bullet, muzzle velocity, range, spin drift, and the wind. Wind is smoothed over the last several seconds of speed and direction, longer at longer range, so the hold won't jump with every split-second flicker. That's why it can differ from the Current wind hold.
  2. 2
    Live wind Green dot means the wind meter is connected and streaming.
