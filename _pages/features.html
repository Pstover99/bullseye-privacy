02 / 16Wind, conditions and sensors

Fewer inputs, more shooting

The goal is to capture as many inputs as possible automatically and keep manual input to a minimum. The more sensors you have, the less time you spend futzing with your solver or pointing a device in a particular direction like a cave person before you shoot.

  • Simultaneous sensor connectivity. Connect and stay connected to several sensors at once, merged per reading, with stale readings released automatically.
  • A constant, real-time feed from your Bluetooth sensors: Calypso Ultrasonic, Calypso WeatherDot and WeatherFlow Tempest, plus RuuviTag and most sensors that use the standard Environmental Sensing service.
  • Calypso direction calibration, so the meter can face any direction and doesn't need to be moved.

  • Auto fill of the solution from Bluetooth, and auto-reconnect to saved sensors.
  • Pick the source for wind, temperature, pressure and humidity. If that sensor drops out, the reading goes back to manual rather than silently switching.
  • GPS latitude and compass azimuth captured on the phone, with a GPS power profile you choose. The live heading shows its accuracy, and you can lock the facing once you're set up.
  • Sensor screen with battery and connection state for every device.
03 / 16Ballistic solver

A full point-mass engine

A point-mass trajectory solver using RK4 integration. RK4 traces the trajectory in small increments and checks the rate of change four times during every increment, which is substantially more accurate than one estimate per step. That lets the solver keep updating velocity and position while gravity, aerodynamic drag, wind and Coriolis change throughout the flight.

  • G1 and G7 drag models.
  • Drop, windage, velocity, energy, stability and time of flight at any range, with a full trajectory table. The solver tells you where your bullet goes transonic and subsonic, with its stability and energy on target at that distance.
  • Aerodynamic jump, spin drift and stability factor are calculated and applied, and hit probability is worked out for you.
Show 8 moreShow fewer
  • Coriolis correction from latitude and azimuth, with an automatic-on-launch option.
  • Shot angle sampled from the phone or entered by hand.
  • Atmospherics (temperature, station pressure and humidity) go into the solution, and the density altitude is calculated and displayed.
  • Multiple wind readings blended into the solution, by clock position or absolute bearing.
  • Two-point truing to match your observed dope, plus a fit over the misses in your log.
  • Muzzle velocity by powder temperature, from a table you keep per load.
  • Cold-bore and clean-bore offsets for the first shot. You choose whether the solver treats the first shot as cold bore or clean bore.
  • Zero conditions stored per load, so the zero corrects for a change in conditions.

04 / 16Coriolis, spin drift and jump

The Earth turns under the bullet

The Coriolis effect exists because the Earth rotates underneath the bullet while it's in flight. How much it moves the impact depends on your latitude, your direction of fire, the time of flight and the velocity.

  • Shooting north or south mostly creates a vertical effect.
  • Shooting east or west mostly creates a horizontal effect.
  • Longer flight times increase the correction.

For a typical cartridge at around 1,000 yards, Coriolis is usually measurable. It can be several tenths of a mil, and it matters more the further past 1,000 you push.

Bullseye can automatically determine your GPS latitude, your direction to target, the Coriolis correction, spin drift and aerodynamic jump, and roll them all into the final firing solution. You can also type the latitude and azimuth by hand.

06 / 16Shot log and analysis

Beyond paper DOPE

The app records each shot: hit or miss, where it landed on the target, the distance, the environmental conditions at the time of the shot, and the time. That builds a real-world performance database instead of relying on paper DOPE alone.

  • Shot strings with a per-shot call (hit, miss with location, no-call), distance, name, notes and where each number came from.
  • Aggregate analysis across the strings you select: hit rate by distance, miss location pattern, and cold bore versus warm bore.
  • Dope by density altitude, and predicted versus observed for every shot.

07 / 16Groups and group analysis

See the shape of your group

Record groups three ways: measured from a target photo, entered shot by shot, or entered as the statistics you already have.

  • Group photo measuring: camera or gallery, reference distance calibration, perspective correction, then place and nudge each impact under a fixed crosshair.
  • Group dispersion plot: the app analyzes the horizontal and vertical position of every shot and plots the group's size, shape, direction and center. One and two sigma ellipses, scale rings, the aim point, and holes drawn at caliber scale.
  • The numbers: per-axis standard deviation with bias correction and a 95 percent confidence interval, extreme spread, mean radius, and the group center's offset from your aim point. In inches, centimeters, MOA or mil.

09 / 16Voice shot calls

Stay in the rifle

Optional, and off by default. Speak hit, impact or miss, the direction and how far off the shot was, and it's logged. For example, after your shot you say "miss, three tenths mil left", and the app captures and logs it with the atmospherics and wind at that moment.

  • On-device speech recognition. Audio never leaves the phone and is never written to a file.
  • Impulse blanking and a near-field gate keep the shot itself, and your neighbors on the line, from being heard as calls.
  • A follow-up window for adding a direction after the call. "Undo" or "scratch that" takes the last shot back.

  • Your choice of what the app listens for, and plain words about where the audio goes. It listens only while a solution is on screen.

14 / 16Barrel life, cleaning logs and reminders

Know where the barrel is

Track the full life of the barrel by actual round count against expected round count.

  • Round count per barrel, counted from the shots you log. Add rounds you fired without logging.
  • Cleaning frequency by round count or by calendar, whichever comes first, for routine and deep cleans.
  • Log each clean with everything that was cleaned: chamber, bolt, bore, muzzle device. Shots after a bore clean are tagged clean-bore and kept out of truing.
  • Reminders that tell you when it's time for the next cleaning or the barrel nears its life, plus one-off reminders such as a clean before a match. Off until you turn them on.
