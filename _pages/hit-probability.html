Solver technical detail

How your Hit Probability is worked out

Your Hit Probability is the odds that a shot lands on the target. Bullseye estimates how far each source of error could move the bullet. It combines them into one pattern around your aim point and works out how much of that pattern lands on the target.

Inputs

Today's conditions

Your Hit Probability uses the same inputs as your firing solution:

  • AirToday's temperature, pressure, humidity and altitude. It also uses the air on the day you zeroed, if you entered it.
  • WindThe wind speed and direction.
  • GeometryShot angle, latitude and direction of fire.
  • Muzzle velocityAdjusted for powder temperature, cold or clean bore, barrel wear and truing.

Conditions don't raise or lower your Hit Probability by themselves, because the pattern is centered on your solution. What they change is how much each error costs.

Thinner air
Thinner air means less drift per mph.
Stronger wind
A stronger wind makes a wrong direction call cost more.
Longer shot
A longer shot makes every error cost more.

Your spread

Your own accuracy

Bullseye also needs to know how tightly you and your rifle group. It uses the best source you have, in this order:

  1. 1

    Your measured groups Shown as: measured

    These are groups with this rifle and load, five shots or more each. One group is enough to start, and the result is marked provisional until you have three.

    Bullseye removes the part of the spread your velocity variation already explains, so it isn't counted twice. It uses how tight your groups are, not where they landed.

  2. 2

    Your called shots Shown as: from called shots

    With no measured group yet, Bullseye uses the misses in your shot log. It counts warm-bore shots only, and only misses where you said how far off the shot was. It needs at least five each way, up and down and left and right.

    A called miss already includes that shot's velocity and wind error, so those aren't added again.

  3. 3

    An estimate Shown as: estimated

    With neither, Bullseye assumes 1 MOA each way and marks the result "estimated."

Measured groups always win over called shots, and the two are never mixed. Once you have one five-shot group, your shot log no longer changes this number.

Measure your groups so your Hit Probability uses your real accuracy.

  • 5-shot groups minimum
  • 10-shot groups are better
  • 20-shot groups are honest
