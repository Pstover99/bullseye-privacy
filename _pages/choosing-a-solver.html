Choosing a solver

Everyone makes a solver now. Elephant in the room

There are more ballistic solver apps than ever, and most of them will give you an accurate hold. So an accurate hold is no longer the question. The question is what else a solver does for you, who stands behind it, and what it ties you to.

These are the questions we'd ask of any solver, ours included.

Is there a real company behind it?

A solver is something you trust with every shot. Before you trust it, look at who makes it. Is there a company name on it? A support address that a person answers? A website with real depth about how the solver works, or just an app store listing and a few screenshots?

If you can't find out how it works or who to ask, that tells you something.

Beyond an accurate hold, what does it do for you?

Accurate holds are table stakes now. What sets a solver apart is the time it saves you and how fast it helps you improve. Does it learn from what your rifle actually did? Does it tell you why a shot missed? Does it keep your data in one place, or leave you with a notebook, a spreadsheet and three apps?

Bullseye

Every shot you log, with the conditions at that moment, trues the solver to your rifle. Sagitta Ballistics AI answers questions from your own shots, loads and groups, and tells you where your misses come from. Measure a group from a photo, set up a match from the match book, and call shots by voice without coming off the rifle.

Does it lock you into one brand's gear?

Some solvers only work within a proprietary company controlled ecosystem. This works great for the company but always works out poorly for the consumer. That means compromises: the wind meter you want doesn't connect, or the one that does costs more, or a better device comes out and you can't use it. This is not the future.

Bullseye

An open device platform. It works with Calypso wind meters and weather sensors, the WeatherFlow Tempest, RuuviTag, and most sensors that use the standard Bluetooth Environmental Sensing service. Several at once, merged reading by reading. Any maker ready to integrate is welcome.

Does every device need its own solver?

When the solver lives inside the wind meter, the rangefinder and the phone, every one of them needs your rifle and load. Change a muzzle velocity and you have to sync it everywhere. Sync rarely works as smoothly as promised, and a device that missed the update gives you the wrong hold without telling you.

Bullseye

One solver, on your phone. The sensors send readings to it, and the External Display shows the solution it sends. Your rifles and loads live in one place, so there's nothing to sync between devices and no copy to go stale.

The phone in your pocket has far more processing power and better components than any wind meter or rangefinder, and it's always with you. Let the other devices do what they do best: measure distance, angle, direction of fire, wind speed and wind direction, and feed those readings to the solver. Putting a copy of the solver in each of them is redundant. It's profitable for the companies that sell them, but it costs you more, leaves you guessing which device has the right numbers and restricts your gear options.

Is the hardware keeping up?

The phone in your pocket has a faster processor, a bigger and brighter screen, GPS, a compass and a camera. Many solver devices were designed years ago and stay the same for years after.

Bullseye

Runs on the phone you already carry and gets better with updates. Pair it with today's sensors, not last decade's.

Does it work when you have no signal?

Ranges and hunting ground are often out of service. A solver that needs a connection, or an account, fails you exactly where you need it.

Bullseye

The solver, sensors, shot log, groups, matches and the External Display all work with no connection. Sign-in, cloud backup and Sagitta are optional.

The usual way, or a better one?

A lot of shooters run the same setup because everyone at the line runs it. That doesn't make it the best way. It only makes it the familiar one.

Ask the six questions above of the solver you use now. If it comes up short, try something built for how you shoot today.
